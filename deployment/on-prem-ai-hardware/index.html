    On-Prem, Local AI & Hardware¶

    AutoAgentAI is built so it can run entirely on the dealership's own premises, on local hardware, with local AI models, so that customer data never leaves the building. This is not a bolt-on — it is a first-class deployment mode, and for most dealerships it is the recommended one, because the data involved is exactly the kind that privacy law treats as sensitive (see Privacy, PII & Future-Proofing).

    The core promise

    Everything the agent needs — the models that read and reason, the knowledge base, the customer data, the decision-making — can live on a machine in the back office. The only things that cross the network are the things you explicitly choose to send out (e.g. a manufacturer feedback summary), and even those are on your terms.

    Why on-prem is now realistic¶

    Two things changed:

    1. Open-source local models got good enough. The open models you can run on a single workstation today do the specific jobs this product needs — reading notes, classifying intent, summarizing, extracting structure, ranking options, drafting language — at a quality that was cloud-only a short time ago. See Local Models Are Enough.
    2. Agentic AI matured. The orchestration, tool-use, and memory patterns that make an autonomous agent work no longer require a frontier cloud model for every step. The heavy reasoning can be reserved, batched, or done locally.

    Together, these make a fully local, private, autonomous customer-intelligence agent buildable on dealership hardware — which a few years ago it was not.

    The four deployment options¶

    AutoAgentAI ships as four tiers, from cheapest-but-not-private to fully sovereign. A dealer picks based on how sensitive their data is, what they can spend, and how much they want to keep in-house.

    Tier 0 — Cloud (cheap, fast, not PII-compliant)¶

    Uses cloud AI services and cloud storage. No or minimal on-site hardware. It is the cheapest and quickest way to stand the system up and prove it works — and it can genuinely work. The catch is unavoidable: customer data leaves the premises and passes through third-party cloud and AI providers, so it is not suitable for PII-regulated customer data. Use it for pilots, demos, non-sensitive workloads, or proving value before investing in hardware — with eyes open about the trade-off (see Cloud vs On-Prem: The Risks).

    Tier 1 — Local Essentials (entry on-prem, private)¶

    A single on-prem node — a capable mini-server or workstation — runs small-to-mid local models and holds the data locally. Data never leaves the building. Modest cost, modest footprint; ideal for a single rooftop that wants privacy from day one and a real but contained workload.

    Tier 2 — Local Pro (dealership-grade, private)¶

    A proper on-prem GPU server/workstation runs mid-to-large local models with a local knowledge base and vector store, driving a full fleet of kiosks and iPads across the dealership. Handles the real concurrent load of a busy showroom. This is the sweet spot for most single dealerships that take their data seriously.

    Tier 3 — Local Enterprise (group / flagship, sovereign)¶

    A GPU cluster with high availability, redundancy, and the largest local models, serving a dealer group or multiple sites. Data sovereignty across the whole group, auditable at scale, with edge-sync between locations. For organizations where the data is strategic and downtime is unacceptable.

    Same product, different engine

    All four tiers run the same AutoAgentAI — same pillars, same interfaces, same agent. What changes is where the compute and data live and how much of it you can run at once. A dealer can start at Tier 0 to prove value, then move the exact same workflows on-prem at Tier 1–3 when ready.

    Interfaces: kiosks and iPads¶

    The system meets people where they are, with two complementary interfaces:

    • McDonald's-style self-serve kiosks. Large, friendly, touch-first terminals — the same pattern customers already trust from quick-service restaurants. Low intimidation, self-paced, and they capture rich interaction data as a natural by-product of being genuinely useful to the customer.
    • iPads. The mobile, handheld form of the same system. Crucially, iPads are generally the nodes that connect to the whole system — a salesperson or greeter carries one, and through it the whole local AutoAgentAI (models, data, agent) is at hand anywhere on the lot or in the showroom.

    Outbound (optional, chosen) Aggregated OEM feedback, group sync Egress on your terms

    Hardware guidance (illustrative)¶

    Exact specifications are a Phase-1 spec decision and depend on chosen models and load. As rough orientation only:

    • Tier 1: a single modern workstation with one capable GPU and ample RAM/SSD — enough for small/mid local models and a single site's light, interactive load.
    • Tier 2: a dedicated GPU server (one or more workstation/data-center GPUs), fast NVMe storage, and a UPS — enough for larger models and a showroom's full concurrent kiosk + iPad load.
    • Tier 3: multiple GPU servers in a rack with redundancy, high-availability, and replicated storage — for dealer-group scale and uptime guarantees.

    Numbers here are orientation, not a quote

    Treat all hardware and cost statements as directional. Final sizing comes with the specifications once models and expected load are fixed.
