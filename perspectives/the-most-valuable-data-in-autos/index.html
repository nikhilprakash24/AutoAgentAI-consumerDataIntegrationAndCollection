    Capturing the Car Industry's Most Valuable Data¶

    We are not inventing a new kind of AI. Everyone has heard of AI agents; everyone is building them; agentic AI is not a secret. What's different is where we point it. We are in the automotive industry, and we are using the current advances in AI, agentic AI, and open-source local models to do one specific thing nobody has done well: capture and use the single most valuable data in the entire car industry — the customer's real intent, behaviour, and decision-making.

    The one idea

    The technology is ordinary. The target is extraordinary. An AI agent — the thing everyone already knows about — aimed at the richest, least-captured data in autos, running privately on local models, closes a gap the whole industry has.

    The tech is known; the application is the edge¶

    flowchart LR
    T["Known technology<br/><small>AI · agentic AI · open local models</small>"]
    D["Unclaimed data<br/><small>customer intent, behaviour, decisions</small>"]
    T --> X((AutoAgentAI))
    D --> X
    X --> V["The valuable, defensible position"]
    • AI agents — widely understood, widely available.
    • Agentic patterns (planning, tools, memory) — mature and documented.
    • Open-source local models — now good enough to run the whole thing privately on-prem (Local Models Are Enough).

    Individually, none of that is a moat. Pointed at the car industry's customer behaviour data, and able to actually capture it, it is. The moat is the data and the capture, not the model.

    Why this data is the prize¶

    The automotive purchase is unusually rich in signal and unusually high in stakes:

    • It's a big, considered, emotional purchase — months of deliberation, lots of behaviour to read.
    • It's multi-channel — web, showroom, test drive, service, finance.
    • It recurs over a long relationship — purchase, service, trade-in, repurchase.
    • The decisions are high-value — a single correctly-read customer is a car sold, a trade-in captured, a service relationship kept.

    And almost none of it is captured today, because capturing it was impossible for the people on the floor (Why Salespeople Can't Capture This Data). The most valuable data in the industry is sitting in plain sight, uncollected.

    What "capture and utilize" means here¶

    We don't just store the data — the whole point is the loop from capture to use:

    flowchart LR
    CAP[Capture<br/><small>kiosks, iPads, all channels</small>] --> UNI[Unify<br/><small>one profile per customer</small>]
    UNI --> INS[Insight<br/><small>intent, blockers, propensity</small>]
    INS --> ACT[Act<br/><small>the right help at the right moment</small>]
    ACT --> FB[Feed back<br/><small>dealer → and up to the OEM</small>]
    FB --> CAP

    Everyone can run an agent. Few can capture this data at all, and fewer can turn it into the right action and feed it back up the chain (see Closing the Loop to the OEM). That capture → use → feedback loop is the product.

    Why now, and why us¶

    Enabler Why it matters
    Agentic AI matured An "analyst that never sleeps" is finally buildable
    Local models got good enough The whole thing runs privately on-prem — essential for this PII-heavy data
    Privacy pressure rising Sovereign, on-prem capture is the only durable way to hold this data (Privacy)
    We're in the industry We know where the signal is, how dealerships work, and what the OEM wants

    The positioning

    "AI agent" is table stakes. An AI agent that captures and uses the most valuable, least-captured data in the car industry — privately, on local models, all the way up to the OEM — is the thing. We're not selling the hammer; we're the ones who found the gold it's for.
