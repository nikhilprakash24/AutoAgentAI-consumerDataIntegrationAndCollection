    From Raw Data to a Decision: A Dealership Scenario¶

    Every claim in these docs comes down to one motion: raw data → useful data → insight → action → outcome. This article walks that motion end to end, with a single concrete dealership scenario, so the abstract pillars become something you can picture happening on a Tuesday afternoon.

    flowchart LR
    R[Raw data] --> U[Useful data] --> I[Insight] --> A[Action] --> O[Outcome]
    O -.feedback.-> R

    The four transformations¶

    Stage What it is What it takes
    Raw data Everything that happened, unstructured and scattered Capture (kiosks, iPads, systems)
    Useful data The same events, unified and resolved per customer Customer Integration
    Insight A grounded, tailored finding that implies a decision Insights Engine + Behaviour Models
    Action A specific, measured next-best-action Decision Intelligence

    The scenario: meet "Maria"¶

    1 · Raw data (what actually happened)¶

    Over six weeks, Maria generates signal nobody is joining up:

    • Browses the dealership site three evenings, lingering on a mid-size SUV and a hybrid trim; configures a build but doesn't submit.
    • Walks in on a Saturday. A greeter with an iPad checks her in; she mentions she has "a second kid on the way" and is "worried about running costs."
    • Test-drives the SUV. The salesperson's notes (dictated into the iPad): "liked space, hesitated on fuel, asked about resale."
    • Opens two follow-up emails but clicks neither offer.
    • Leaves a 4-star service review on her current, aging car two weeks later: "love the team, car's getting tired."

    Today, those five things live in five systems and tell no one a story.

    2 · Useful data (unified and resolved)¶

    AutoAgentAI's integration layer resolves all five to one customer, Maria, on-prem:

    flowchart LR
    W[Web: SUV + hybrid builds] --> P((Maria))
    V[Visit: 2nd child, running-cost worry] --> P
    T[Test drive: space yes, fuel hesitation, resale Q] --> P
    E[Email: opens, no clicks] --> P
    S[Service review: aging car, loves team] --> P

    Now it's not five fragments — it's a person with a trajectory.

    3 · Insight (grounded and tailored)¶

    The behaviour models and insights engine read the unified picture and surface a custom insight, not a generic metric:

    Insight

    Maria is a high-propensity hybrid-SUV buyer whose single blocker is running cost, and whose current car is near end-of-life. Customers matching this pattern (growing family + fuel hesitation + aging trade-in + service loyalty) convert at well-above-average rates when shown a total-cost-of-ownership comparison and a trade-in value up front — and churn to a competitor when left to a generic follow-up. Evidence: her web/visit/drive/review signals + the dealership's own history of similar customers. Confidence: high. Expected impact: one incremental hybrid sale + a trade-in.

    No salesperson assembled this. It was impossible for one to (see Why Salespeople Can't Capture This Data).

    4 · Action (a specific next-best-action)¶

    Decision Intelligence turns the insight into a concrete, guardrailed action — not a vague "follow up":

    • Next-best-action: a personalized follow-up that leads with a total cost-of-ownership comparison (addressing the exact blocker) and an up-front trade-in estimate for her aging car.
    • Routed to her salesperson's iPad with the reasoning and talking points, framed as assurance, not pressure (see Reinventing the Salesperson's Role).
    • Guardrails: within contact-frequency limits and her consent; a human sends it.

    5 · Outcome (measured, and fed back)¶

    The action is tracked against a holdout. Maria books a second drive, trades in, and buys the hybrid. The outcome feeds back: the models learn that this pattern + this action worked, sharpening the next recommendation — and, in aggregate, this signal can flow to the OEM (see Closing the Loop to the OEM).

    Why each stage needs the system¶

    flowchart TB
    R[Raw data<br/><small>scattered across 5 systems</small>] -->|integration| U[Useful data<br/><small>one resolved Maria</small>]
    U -->|models + engine| I[Insight<br/><small>blocker = running cost</small>]
    I -->|decision intelligence| A[Action<br/><small>TCO + trade-in, as assurance</small>]
    A -->|attribution| O[Outcome<br/><small>sale + trade-in, learned from</small>]

    Remove any stage and the value collapses: raw data with no integration is noise; integrated data with no insight is a nicer database; an insight with no action is a chart nobody acts on; an action with no measured outcome never improves. The agent's job is to run this whole motion, continuously, for every customer at once.

    Illustrative

    "Maria" and the numbers are an illustration of the mechanism, not a case study or a performance claim.
